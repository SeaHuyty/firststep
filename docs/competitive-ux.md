# How comparable products serve first-time investors

Researched 17 Oct 2026 to decide FirstStep's screens for people with little investing knowledge or confidence with numbers. Each finding links to its source. The last column says what FirstStep takes from it; the decisions are recorded with their research in `ux-psychology.md`.

| Product or study | What they do | What FirstStep takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wealthfront** (US robo-advisor) | New users pick their experience level; a short list of goals avoids choice paralysis; benefit-led copy ("retire when you want, how you want"); the Path chart updates as inputs change; "Why this risk score?" sits next to the recommendation [W1]. The risk questionnaire asks the minimum, checks answers for consistency, and weights the more conservative score more heavily [W2]. | A plain "why" beside every recommendation, one clear next step per screen, conservative scoring (already ours). |
| **Betterment** (US) | Recommends risk from goal and horizon, testing outcomes across poor-to-average markets weighted by likelihood [B1]. | Outcomes as ranges, never one number (already ours). |
| **Acorns** (US micro-investing) | Familiar metaphors ("spare change"); plain microcopy; each step leads to the next; a recommended-portfolio reveal; animated success checks [A1, A2]. Critics note decoration can hurt legibility [A3]. | A calm "your risk level" reveal saying what it means for your money; motion as feedback, not decoration. |
| **Nutmeg / J.P. Morgan Personal Investing** (UK) | Ten risk levels tied to the share in stocks; projections at 3 and 10 years include a bad case in money with its odds ("about a 14% loss ... one in 20") [N1]. | The bad case in dollars, and odds as "X out of 100". |
| **Ant Fortune "帮你投"** (China, with Vanguard) | Ongoing companionship (陪伴): investment notes every two days, daily and weekly commentary and live streams; the platform reports readers chased gains and panic-sold less (self-reported) [AF1, AF2]. Reuses the risk profile Alipay already has [AF3]. | Sprout writes a short year-in-review when you fast-forward, and coaches through drops. |
| **FCA behavioural testing of risk warnings** (UK regulator) | "Your capital is at risk" often fails to convey that money can be lost, especially for newer investors. Behaviourally informed, salient warnings with a "take 2 minutes to learn more" link raised comprehension by 6–10 percentage points [F1, F2]. | Concrete loss examples in the person's own dollars; a two-minute "how this works" explainer; never a bare disclaimer. |
| **Robinhood** (anti-pattern, in the handoff research) | Confetti on trades was cited by regulators as gamification of novices. | No celebration of trades; calm receipts. |
